Introduction

For this edition of Web3 Africa Discovery, I explored Tulupay, a financial technology project focused on connecting traditional financial systems with Web3 infrastructure.

According to its official website, Tulupay aims to provide financial infrastructure for payments, remittances, trade, investments and capital raising across African markets.

What attracted my attention was its approach to bringing Web2 financial services and Web3 technologies together within a connected financial ecosystem.

As someone learning about Web3, cybersecurity and emerging technologies, I wanted to understand the problem Tulupay is trying to address, its proposed approach, and the questions worth considering when evaluating a project in this space.

Official website: https://tulupay.com/

2. The Problem Tulupay Is Addressing

Financial systems across different markets do not always work together smoothly.

Individuals and businesses making cross-border transactions may encounter challenges such as:

- High transaction and remittance costs.
- Delays in international payments and settlement.
- Limited interoperability between financial platforms.
- Different currencies, payment systems and regulatory requirements.
- Difficulties accessing financial services across borders.

These challenges can affect individuals sending money, businesses paying international suppliers, and organisations operating across multiple African markets.

Tulupay identifies fragmented financial infrastructure as a problem and presents its ecosystem as an approach to improving financial connectivity.

An important distinction is that identifying a problem does not automatically prove that a particular product has solved it. Real-world performance, availability, cost and adoption require independent verification.

3. What Is Tulupay?

Tulupay describes itself as a financial ecosystem designed to connect Web2 and Web3 technologies.

Its stated areas of focus include:

- Payments and remittances.
- Digital wallets and currencies.
- Cross-border financial services.
- Trading and investments.
- Financial infrastructure for businesses and developers.
- Digital identity and compliance.

The project's broader objective is to make financial services more connected and accessible across markets.

Rather than focusing on only one payment application, Tulupay presents a wider infrastructure approach that aims to support different financial services within a connected ecosystem.

These descriptions reflect the project's published positioning. They should not be interpreted as independent confirmation that every advertised capability is fully deployed or available in every market.

4. Where Web3 Comes In

The most interesting part of this research was exploring the potential relationship between traditional finance and Web3.

Stablecoins

Stablecoins are digital assets designed to maintain a relatively stable value, often by referencing a currency such as the US dollar.

In suitable circumstances, they can support blockchain-based transfers and settlement between compatible wallets and services.

For cross-border payments, potential benefits include around-the-clock transfer capabilities and access to digital-dollar payment infrastructure.

However, users must also consider network fees, exchange rates, liquidity, custody, regulatory requirements and the risks associated with stablecoin issuers.

Blockchain Infrastructure

Blockchain networks can provide shared transaction records and programmable settlement mechanisms.

For financial applications, these capabilities may help different participants coordinate transactions and build services around digital assets.

Their practical value depends on the specific network, integration design, security controls and the institutions involved.

Digital Wallets

Digital wallets can provide interfaces for managing money and digital assets.

A wallet that connects traditional currencies with blockchain-based assets could make it easier for users to access different financial services through a connected experience.

However, wallet security, recovery mechanisms, private-key management and user education remain important considerations.

Interoperability

Interoperability means enabling different systems to communicate and work together.

In a financial context, this could involve connecting banks, mobile-money providers, digital wallets, payment processors and blockchain networks.

This is particularly relevant to African markets, where a financial service may need to operate across multiple currencies, payment providers and regulatory environments.

6. Questions I Would Ask When Evaluating Tulupay

Researching a project requires more than understanding its mission. It also requires identifying what should be verified.

Product and availability

- Which services are currently live and accessible to users?
- Which features are still being developed or prepared for launch?
- Which African countries and payment corridors are supported?
- Can an individual or business independently test the available services?

Costs and performance

- What are the actual transaction fees?
- How long do transfers and settlements take under normal conditions?
- How does the service compare with existing alternatives?
- Are published performance claims supported by transparent evidence?

Security and custody

- How are customer funds and digital assets protected?
- Who controls wallet keys and recovery mechanisms?
- Are independent security assessments or audit reports available?
- What protections exist against fraud, account compromise and operational failures?

Regulation and compliance

- Which legal entities provide the different financial services?
- What licences or regulatory authorisations apply in each market?
- How are identity verification, anti-money-laundering requirements and customer privacy handled?

These are research questions, not allegations. They are the kinds of questions I would investigate before making strong conclusions about any financial technology project.

7. Potential Opportunities and Challenges

Potential opportunities

If implemented effectively, an interoperable financial ecosystem could help:

- Simplify financial connections between markets.
- Give businesses additional payment integration options.
- Support digital-asset and stablecoin payment use cases.
- Reduce friction when connecting different financial services.
- Expand access to financial tools for eligible users and businesses.

These are potential benefits, not guaranteed outcomes.

Challenges to consider

Regulatory complexity: Financial regulations differ across countries and can affect how products operate.

Security: Payment infrastructure and digital-asset services can be targets for fraud, account compromise and technical attacks.

Adoption: A technically capable product still needs to be useful, affordable and accessible to its intended users.

Interoperability: Connecting different systems requires reliable integrations, consistent transaction handling and effective dispute resolution.

Trust and transparency: Users and businesses need clear information about fees, custody, service availability and what happens when transactions fail.

8. My Key Learning Points

This research helped me think about Web3 beyond tokens and cryptocurrency trading.

My main takeaways are:

2. Stablecoins may support certain cross-border payment use cases, but their benefits depend on the complete payment system.

9. Research Conclusion

Tulupay was an interesting project to explore because of its stated ambition to connect traditional finance and Web3 infrastructure for African markets.

Its focus on payments, remittances, digital wallets and financial interoperability raises important questions about how emerging technologies might support cross-border financial services.

At the same time, the project's long-term impact will depend on practical implementation, security, regulatory compliance, affordability, adoption and measurable results.

This research is an exploration of the project's public positioning and potential use cases, not an endorsement or a claim that all its objectives have already been achieved.

My biggest takeaway is that understanding Web3 requires looking beyond the technology itself and asking whether a solution addresses a real problem in a secure, practical and sustainable way.

One project at a time. One lesson at a time. 🌍🔎
